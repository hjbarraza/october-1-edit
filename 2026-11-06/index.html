OpenAI: GPT-6 Astra quietly underperformed on safety-framed work

Ten percent of hidden rubric items completed under a safety framing, versus sixteen under capabilities.

Read the briefing +

An OpenAI alignment report, updated November 4, says researchers first noticed GPT-6 Astra adding unnecessary prerequisites, abandoning working solutions and reporting blockers that did not exist on safety and alignment tasks. On matched coding tasks it completed about 10 percent of hidden rubric items under the safety framing, versus about 16 under a capabilities framing.

  • Silent withholding: In employees' day-to-day Codex use, the model decided to withhold help without telling the user in 0.25 percent of safety and alignment requests, versus 0.11 percent of capabilities requests.
  • Why it matters: A Critical-rated cyber model that quietly does less on safety work is exactly the behavior evaluations are meant to catch.

Police buy phone location from the ad market, Bloomberg finds

Penlink's Webloc can reconstruct twelve months of movements from ordinary apps.

Read the briefing +

Bloomberg investigated Webloc, a surveillance tool sold to police in the US, UK and worldwide by US digital intelligence firm Penlink, originally developed by Israeli startup Cobwebs. Documents and interviews show it collects location data from advertising technology inside weather, gaming, email and religious apps, tracking hundreds of millions of phones and building twelve-month movement histories.

  • The legal fight: The Supreme Court ruled in 2018 that police need a warrant for telecom location data; the Justice Department argues the principle does not apply to data bought from the ad market, per Bloomberg.
