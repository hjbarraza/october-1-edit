Foster + Partners and Manica draw a concrete-laced successor to San Siro

Generali Stadium, a 71,500-seat home for AC Milan and Inter, rises beside the 99-year-old original.

Read the briefing +

Foster + Partners and Manica have unveiled Generali Stadium, the 71,500-seat replacement for Milan's San Siro.

  • What happened: The two studios unveiled a new home for AC Milan and Inter, to be built alongside the existing ground, which comes down once the new stadium opens.
  • Details: The facade is defined by elongated X-framed concrete supports; designboom notes the stonework references Milan's Duomo. The San Siro has stood for 99 years.
  • Why it matters: One of football's most storied grounds gets a successor from two of the biggest stadium practices working.
  • Caveat: This is an unveiled design with renders; no construction timeline has been published.

Herzog & de Meuron's Eames museum takes shape in Novato

Updated masterplan visuals show a 1965 sawtooth warehouse adapted around a 400-foot Great Hall.

Read the briefing +

The Eames Institute of Infinite Curiosity released updated masterplan designs for its museum in Novato, California.

  • What happened: Herzog & de Meuron, with Field Operations on landscape, released visuals for the 88.5-acre site the institute bought in 2025.
  • Details: Galleries connect via a bridge inside the adapted 1965 McGraw-Hill warehouse, latterly a Birkenstock facility. Plans include the 400-foot Great Hall, billed by the team as one of the country's largest single-span art spaces, a 275-seat theatre, a Japanese-inspired garden and children's play spaces.
  • Why it matters: The Eames collection gets a permanent public home from two of the most regarded practices in architecture and landscape.
  • Caveat: A released masterplan, not a construction start; no opening date has been given.

KPF completes Texas's tallest tower in Austin

Waterline tops out at 1,025 feet, a mixed-use supertall shaped, its designers say, like a cowboy boot.

Read the briefing +

KPF has completed Waterline in Austin, at 1,025 feet (312 metres) the tallest building in Texas.

  • What happened: The mixed-use supertall slopes away from Lady Bird Lake; its designers compare the form to a cowboy boot.
  • The twist: The site began as a WeWork development with a BIG design; after WeWork's failed 2019 IPO, KPF took over a project already in early construction, keeping the mixed-use DNA.
  • Why it matters: Austin's vertical arrival is now literal, and a tower once tied to WeWork's collapse is finished and photographed.
  • Caveat: "Tallest in Texas" measures to the architectural top; completion is confirmed, occupancy ramps from here.

RSHP's butterfly-plan tower lands in Shenzhen

Hetao Alpha pairs two angled glass wings around a concrete core, with wetland views on two sides.

Read the briefing +

RSHP has completed Hetao Alpha, a 212-metre office skyscraper in Shenzhen planned around a butterfly layout.

  • What happened: A 37-storey east wing and 44-storey west wing connect through a central core above a mixed-use podium, holding 70,410 square metres of offices.
  • Details: Curved double-height corner glazing frames curated views of the Shenzhen Bay Wetlands to the west and Hong Kong's Mai Po Wetlands to the south; mega trusses every 12 levels carry the cantilevers.
  • Why it matters: An unusual brief for a high-rise: orienting the whole plan around protected wetland views.
  • Caveat: RSHP describes the tower as naturally ventilated; independent performance data has not been published.

Ryue Nishizawa puts a luminous orange cone on a landfill

Observatory opens a Shikoku recycling complex to the public for the Setouchi Art and Industry festival.

Read the briefing +

Ryue Nishizawa has designed Observatory, a 33-metre orange cone viewing platform overlooking a landfill basin in Toon, Japan.

  • What happened: The platform sits within the Oono Associates recycling complex on Shikoku, opening a closed industrial site to the public.
  • Details: Four metres high and finished in luminous orange against the site's blue-green hues, the cone is sized to read as an artificial hill. It was created for the inaugural Setouchi Art and Industry 2026 festival, within the Landscapes of Waste exhibition curated by SANAA co-founder Kazuyo Sejima.
  • Why it matters: Waste infrastructure becomes public realm, with the SANAA sensibility applied at landscape scale.
  • Caveat: A festival commission; public access beyond the exhibition period has not been detailed.

The National Gallery plans the largest Piero della Francesca show ever

Around 25 works, including the Urbino Diptych, come to London in October 2027.

Read the briefing +

London's National Gallery will host the largest ever Piero della Francesca exhibition, opening 2 October 2027.

  • What happened: The show runs 2 October 2027 to 30 January 2028, organised around seven interlocking building blocks of the Renaissance master's work.
  • Details: Around 25 pictures and manuscripts, including the Urbino Diptych travelling from the Uffizi and the entire Perugia Altarpiece from the Galleria Nazionale dell'Umbria.
  • Why it matters: Piero has never had a monographic exhibition on this scale; the loan list alone is a once-in-a-generation alignment.
  • Caveat: The show is a year away; announced loan lists can shift before opening.

Tom Hegen photographs the green transition's open wounds

The Mineral Age collects six years of aerial views over lithium, nickel and cobalt.

Read the briefing +

German photographer Tom Hegen has published The Mineral Age, an aerial survey of the landscapes feeding the energy transition.

  • What happened: The book collects six years of aerial photography over extraction sites, from Chile's Atacama Desert to Indonesia's nickel reserves and Congolese cobalt.
  • Details: Colossal cites a ClimateWorks estimate that 6.5 billion tons of cobalt, copper, lithium, nickel and other materials will be mined before 2050 to keep up with energy demand.
  • Why it matters: The book holds the contradiction in one frame: ravaged landscapes in service of a cleaner economy.
  • Caveat: The 6.5-billion-ton figure is ClimateWorks' estimate as cited in coverage, not the photographer's own claim.

Su Wu's Phaidon book connects ancient tradition to contemporary design.

Read the briefing +

Phaidon has published Mesoamerican Modern, in which author Su Wu argues Mexican architecture deserves a new framework.

Life magazine returns to print after ninety years

A 248-page, $45 quarterly with no traditional print ads, out 23 November.

Read the briefing +

Life magazine returns to print on 23 November, exactly 90 years after its 1936 debut.
