Read the briefing +

A close viewing of the Bayeux Tapestry at the British Museum finds a medieval epic that remains funny, rude and oddly familiar.

  • Object: The seventy-metre embroidery, on loan from France, tells the story of the Norman invasion of England in ten sixty-six.
  • Detail: A long-running academic count puts its penis tally at ninety-four, with one disputed addition; the larger point is how little human nature has moved on.
  • Tone: Between Halley’s Comet, battle scenes and daily life, the tapestry reads like a stitched serial rather than a solemn relic.
