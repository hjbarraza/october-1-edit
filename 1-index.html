Anthropic pitches a federal gate for catastrophic-risk models

A draft bill would let independent evaluators trigger a court request to block a model release.

Read the briefing +

Anthropic is shopping a draft AI safety bill that would create a legal tripwire before certain frontier models ship.

  • Threshold: The draft describes a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than 50 deaths or more than $1 billion in property damage from biological weapons, loss of control, or a cyberattack.
  • Mechanism: Independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department would have 72 hours to report a qualifying risk, after which Commerce would have to ask a Washington judge to halt distribution.
  • Tension: Evaluators could be paid by AI labs if payment is not contingent on the result, leaving the independence question open.
  • Caveat: This is a draft proposal reported by Politico, not introduced legislation. Anthropic confirmed the draft to Politico but did not answer TechCrunch before publication.

Meta Muse share links exposed private AI chats

Researchers found thousands of shared Muse conversations because conversation titles appeared in discoverable subdomains.

Read the briefing +

Meta's Muse made shared AI conversations easier to find than their users expected.

  • Discovery: Claroc researchers enumerated about 6,800 Muse share links and found executive-search notes, revenue reviews, loan details, property figures and staff calendars in a sample.
  • Cause: Muse put conversation titles into subdomains under cf.metaaiusercontent.com, letting passive DNS records make clicked share links searchable.
  • Response: Meta said it has paused the sharing feature while it reviews the report and considers changes.
  • Caveat: This did not make every Muse conversation public. The exposed pages were shared links whose subdomains were recorded after someone clicked them.

The System One-style model returns typed values directly instead of generating tokens.

Read the briefing +

Eight compromised TLS certificates put the Internet on alert

eMudhra intermediate certificates had private keys weak enough to let others mint trusted credentials.

Read the briefing +

Security teams are racing to contain eight intermediate certificates whose private keys were only three to eight bits long.

  • Response: Browser makers and major apps began blocking the certificates after a signed proof showed an outside party controlled one key.
  • Exposure: The same keys could have signed unauthorized certificates trusted by millions of servers, VPN clients and apps.
  • Long tail: No malicious use has appeared in certificate transparency logs so far, but Linux distributions, embedded systems and IoT devices will take longer to update.
  • Caveat: Exploitation still requires an active adversary-in-the-middle position. The incident is developing.

Recently fixed Plex bugs are being exploited

Attackers are chaining four vulnerabilities to install cryptominers and residential proxy software.

Read the briefing +

A published Plex exploit chain has moved quickly from proof of concept to active attacks.

  • Chain: The Plex2Shell sequence combines unauthenticated SSRF, a loopback pivot, token theft, payload delivery and execution.
  • Observed attacks: A honeypot saw scanning and successful infections within a day, with attackers installing a cryptominer or residential proxy software.
  • Fix: Plex users should run version 1.43.3 and avoid exposing outdated servers directly to the Internet.
  • Caveat: Plex had already fixed the bugs. The current story is exploitation of unpatched systems, not a newly disclosed vendor flaw.

MIT's robo-puffin swims and flies

The sub-300-gram prototype can dive underwater, swim, then leap back into the air.

Read the briefing +

MIT and EPFL engineers have built a flapping-wing robot that can move between water and air.

  • Design: The aerial-aquatic vehicle weighs under 300 grams and uses flexible hydrophobic wings, a waterproof motor and a steerable tail.
  • Tests: Trials in a tank and at Lake Geneva found that medium wings and a steep 70-degree pitch let the robot leave the water without paddling feet.
  • Use: The team sees ocean sampling near icebergs, ports and whale pods, where crewed vessels are costly or unsafe.
  • Caveat: This is a prototype. Turning wings, choppy water and wind remain future tests.

The Stirling Prize winner draws a beige architecture critique

Lion Green Road's award has reopened the argument over whether prize architecture has lost its nerve.

Read the briefing +

This year's Stirling Prize has become a proxy fight over taste, housing and architectural ambition.
