webAI's TwIL-8B tops math benchmarks on a small budget

An 8.8B reasoning model that beats models three times its size on MATH-500.

Read the briefing +

Austin startup webAI released TwIL-8B, an 8.8-billion-parameter reasoning model post-trained from IBM's Granite 4.2 8B with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. Its model card reports 98.2 on MATH-500 and 81.6 on LiveCodeBench, leading open models up to 3.5 times its size on math.

  • The caveat: The card says TwIL-8B trails on knowledge-heavy tests such as GPQA Diamond, and carries no safety tuning beyond its Granite base.
  • The license: Weights are on Hugging Face under webAI's Non-Commercial License 1.0 (the Granite base is Apache 2.0), with a 131K context window and balanced and verbose variants.

Ricursive releases Ripple-65B as open weights

A 65B coding model the company says it built for $32 million.

Read the briefing +

Startup Ricursive released Ripple-65B Preview, a 65-billion-parameter coding model trained from scratch on a 12-trillion-token corpus, with weights and a base checkpoint public on Hugging Face. The company says it scores 71.1 on Terminal-Bench 2, the best among open-weight models on that benchmark, and puts the full R&D cost at $32 million.

  • The hardware: Ricursive says the model fits on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark and ships an NVFP4 checkpoint plus a Colab to get started.
  • The caveat: Benchmark and cost figures are company-reported; license terms were not stated in the materials reviewed.

XPENG-backed researchers open-source GroundingPI

A 4B perception model for physical AI, out ahead of GPT-6 Astra in its own evaluation.

Read the briefing +

Researchers affiliated with XPENG, Peking University, the University of Hong Kong and other institutions released GroundingPI, an open-source visual perception foundation model for physical AI. The roughly 4-billion-parameter model averaged 73.68 across 34 perception benchmarks, ahead of GPT-6 Astra's 71.54 in the research team's own evaluation.

  • The approach: GroundingPI represents scenes as points and bounding boxes across 11 capability categories, from object detection and OCR to robotic spatial localization.
  • The release: Paper, model weights and code are public; the team reports improved robotic manipulation in simulation and better training-data efficiency.

Zyxel zero-day compromises nearly 1,000 switches

GS1900 web-interface flaw; patches shipped in October.

Read the briefing +

GreyNoise is tracking nearly a thousand compromised Zyxel GS1900 switches across 48 countries. Attackers exploited CVE-2026-107441, an out-of-bounds write in the web management interface that lets an unauthenticated attacker with network access run OS commands via crafted HTTP requests, stealing configuration files and credentials.

  • The fix: Zyxel released patches for ten affected models on October 22 and published its advisory November 3; apply them if you run this hardware.
  • The confusion: The flaw was initially mistaken for CVE-2026-7273, an older pre-authentication overflow Zyxel patched in June.

APT29 watering holes hit mobile devices

Fake America250 page, a cloned London luxury hotel, and a Ukrainian analytics supply chain.

Read the briefing +

Sekoia links a large-scale watering-hole campaign to APT29, the intrusion set widely attributed to Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service. Three vectors delivered the same payload: compromised legitimate websites, lookalike domains reverse-proxying high-profile sites, and a supply-chain compromise of a Ukrainian web analytics service that exposed hundreds of sites for a week.

  • The lures: A fake america250.holiday proxied the official US semiquincentennial site, and theowo.club cloned Raffles London at The OWO, the hotel where open-source reporting says President Zelensky stayed on October 14.
  • The payload: Mobile visitors were profiled, then served platform-specific n-day exploit chains including DarkSword, a suspected commercial-surveillance iOS kit; payloads exfiltrate iOS cookies and device data, and Android Chrome data and Wi-Fi passwords, without persistence. Confidence: moderate to high.

NASA redraws the Artemis road map

Artemis III becomes an Earth-orbit docking test in 2027; the landing moves to Artemis IV in 2028.

Read the briefing +

NASA detailed its accelerated Artemis plan: Artemis III is now a 14-day test flight in Earth orbit in 2027, where four astronauts aboard Orion will dock with a Blue Origin Blue Moon lander test article, then undock and dock with a SpaceX Starship Version 3 lander test article. The crewed lunar landing moves to Artemis IV in 2028.

  • The sequence: Blue Origin launches the Blue Moon test article first, SLS launches Orion and its crew, then SpaceX launches the Starship test article for the second docking.
  • The framing: Administrator Jared Isaacman cast the campaign as urgent, citing strategic competitors pursuing a permanent presence beyond Earth.

Swift telescope re-enters after 22 years

A failed private rescue, a final observation in October, and a Sumatra re-entry.

Read the briefing +

NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory re-entered the atmosphere over northern Sumatra on November 4 at 7:39 a.m. EST, 22 years after launch and three months after a private spacecraft was supposed to boost it to a higher, life-extending orbit. Tracker Jonathan McDowell cited US Space Force data for the re-entry.

  • The legacy: Designed for a two-year mission, Swift observed more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts and 1,400 supernovas, and took its final science observation on October 20.
  • The risk: NASA had put the odds of surviving debris hurting anyone at 1 in 2,100; most of the minibus-sized craft was expected to burn up.

NASA buys commercial Ka-band relays

Astranis, GES and Viasat share a combined ceiling above $1 billion.

Read the briefing +

NASA selected three companies to develop Ka-band space relay capability concepts compatible with current missions: Astranis Space Technologies ($264.3 million maximum value), Global Enterprise Solutions ($340.4 million) and Viasat ($460.9 million). The firm-fixed-price contracts run initially through May 3, 2027, with priced options to continue through 2029.

  • The strategy: The work supports NASA's decision to gradually retire the Tracking and Data Relay Satellite fleet and buy communications as one of many commercial customers.
  • The reason: Ka-band services are needed for select in-orbit missions that cannot feasibly modify flight hardware for other commercial services.
