OpenAI's models start designing real hardware

GPT-6 Astra specced a working device: enclosure, PCB, firmware. Mazda signs up.

Read the briefing +

OpenAI says its models are moving from code to atoms: in a November 3 post, it showed GPT-6 Astra designing a complete device (enclosure, printed circuit board, firmware) with two team members steering and no direct human edits, with a contract manufacturer building a small batch of prototypes within a month.

  • The details: The device lets users talk to Codex; OpenAI's hardware teams have grown token usage 23-fold since February, and BenchCAD 2.0 shows a newer internal model scoring 0.875 versus 0.594 for GPT-6 Astra.
  • The caveat: OpenAI says Astra did not consistently account for manufacturer tolerances, and the work with Mazda (aerodynamics and crash safety) and Chip Ganassi Racing remains research collaboration, not production.

Read the briefing +

Critical Node-RED flaw is being exploited

CVSS 10, no authentication needed; Siemens patched in v4.3.4.1.

Read the briefing +

Attackers are exploiting a maximum-severity vulnerability in Node-RED (CVE-2026-58115, CVSS 10), a missing-authentication flaw on the HTTP interface that allows arbitrary code execution with maximum privileges, SecurityWeek reports.

  • The exposure: Siemens disclosed the bug August 11 after finding it in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices; version 4.3.4.1 and later contain the patch. VulnCheck observed successful exploitation from October 30, delivering a Linux bot and webshells.
  • Action: If you run Node-RED, especially in ICS/OT settings, update and keep the editor off the open internet.

An AI attack framework is eating the AI industry's suppliers

WorkOS, Chainstack and Chainalysis disclose breaches tied to ARTEX.

Read the briefing +

An AI offensive-security framework called ARTEX is behind a string of breaches at companies that supply the AI industry itself. WorkOS, whose identity APIs serve OpenAI, Anthropic and Cursor, says an attacker used ARTEX to automate reconnaissance and break into an internal sales application on October 30, taking CRM records and summaries of customer communications.

  • The campaign: Chainstack disclosed a breach that escalated from a development environment into production, exposing customer emails and password hashes and prompting a rotate-your-credentials warning; Chainalysis took its Reactor product offline after detecting 'offensive agentic techniques'. CrowdStrike ties ARTEX to a China-nexus group it calls Vapor Panda, in a campaign touching 200 organizations.
  • The takeaway: WorkOS's own conclusion: AI-driven attacks raise the speed and scale of intrusions, and demand monitoring that connects suspicious activity across systems.

How many launches to fuel a Moon lander?

NASA's refueling graphic lands between 22 and 'six to eight'.

Read the briefing +

NASA published the operational choreography for refueling SpaceX's Starship HLS in Earth orbit ahead of Artemis IV, now the program's first planned crewed lunar landing, SatNews reports.

  • The dispute: Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel member Charlie Precourt cautioned at an October public meeting that fueling the lander could require as many as 22 launches; Elon Musk responded that six to eight will suffice. The gap reflects assumptions on boil-off and transfer efficiency.
  • The plan: Multiple tankers fill an orbital depot; the uncrewed HLS docks, fuels, and departs for the Moon; an initial orbital cryogenic transfer demo precedes uncrewed lunar test flights.
